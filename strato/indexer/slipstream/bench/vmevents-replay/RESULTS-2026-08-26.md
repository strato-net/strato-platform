# Slipstream VM-event replay results — 2026-08-26

## Outcome

The bounded candidate passes the controlled replay gate. Its three-run
processor-plus-database median is 316.800 vmevents/s, 18.55% faster than the
unchanged baseline median of 267.240 vmevents/s. A separate Kafka replay,
including consumption and the final offset commit, processes 20,000 events at
476.237 vmevents/s and finishes at broker-reported lag zero.

The higher of the two measured live VM windows was 79.190 vmevents/s. The
end-to-end candidate is 6.01x that observed pace and 401.15% above its required
1.2x gate of 95.028 vmevents/s.

An earlier aggressive prototype reached a 439.185 vmevents/s median. That is
not the final candidate result: its unbounded output buffering, broad exception
fallback, and same-block history change were removed before the final trials.

## Correctness

`HashTables.sql` compared the row count and deterministic content hash of every
public table. It excludes only the non-key serial `event.id`; it retains the
event primary key and every business field. All six final processor hash files
(Cirrus and transaction-result databases for three runs) were byte-identical to
the unchanged baseline files. The final end-to-end Kafka hashes also matched
the baseline exactly.

The corpus contains three pre-existing updates for fields absent from its
`BlockApps-Mercata` schema: `lpTokenLockVault`, `poolLpSeeder`, and
`tokenLaunchAuction`. Baseline behavior logs and skips these statements. The
candidate preserves that behavior; no other SQL, decode, or processing errors
occurred.

## Bottleneck and optimization

The initial phase profile attributed 68.507 of 73.996 seconds to Cirrus. The
first batching implementation placed 89,431 generated commands in one
transaction. One expected missing-column error aborted it, after which the
entire list was replayed one statement at a time.

The final implementation:

- streams generated outputs through the existing fetch-scoped de-duplicator
  and buffers at most 256 outputs for each database write;
- batches global-event, event-array, and transaction-result inserts;
- splits multi-row inserts at 256 rows or 1 MiB of rendered SQL;
- executes generated Cirrus SQL in transactions bounded by 256 commands or
  2 MiB of rendered SQL;
- retries a failed chunk statement-by-statement only for PostgreSQL data,
  integrity, syntax/access-rule, and PL/pgSQL error classes;
- propagates connection, transaction-rollback, cancellation, and non-SQL
  exceptions; and
- retains the baseline history-trigger behavior and replaces per-event INFO
  logs with batch-level logging and phase metrics.

A single row whose rendered SQL itself exceeds 1 MiB cannot be split without
changing its data and is therefore executed as a singleton. The replay runner
decodes the entire fixed corpus before timing, so its measured RSS is not a
production memory-bound measurement. The new output buffer and rendered SQL are
bounded as above; the pre-existing Kafka fetch list and fetch-scoped
de-duplication set remain outside those bounds so processing order and
de-duplication semantics stay unchanged.
